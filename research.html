Research

Generalizable and human-centered physical AI: from terrestrial manufacturing to space.

Manufacturing is the process of transforming raw materials into useful products in response to human needs and intent. The research vision is to develop generalizable and human-centered physical AI that enables intelligent manufacturing ecosystems to translate human creativity and intent into physical products, autonomously reason about and act on complex physical systems, and expand manufacturing beyond terrestrial environments and resource bases into space.

The guiding principle is that intelligent manufacturing should not be confined to a single machine, factory, environment, or resource base. Together, the three thrusts below work toward intelligent manufacturing ecosystems spanning terrestrial and extraterrestrial environments.

Three connected research thrusts realize this vision, illustrated below.

1. Generalizable and Human-Centered Physical AI for Manufacturing

Intelligent manufacturing systems that enable bidirectional human–machine interaction through natural language, provide understandable predictions and analyses, adaptively orchestrate manufacturing resources, and continuously improve by learning from execution data, outcomes, and human feedback. This thrust realizes the vision for Narrative Manufacturing, centered on two core models: the Large Engineering Model for Manufacturing (LEM²), which interprets human intent and reasons across engineering decisions, and the Manufacturing World Model (MWM), which predicts the physical and operational consequences of those decisions.

2. Human-Robot Collaboration and Heterogeneous Multi-Robot Systems for ISAM

Extends intelligent manufacturing systems to space through Human-Robot Collaboration (HRC) and heterogeneous robotic systems for In-Space Servicing, Assembly, and Manufacturing (ISAM), enabling the autonomous construction, operation, and maintenance of manufacturing systems that leverage vacuum, microgravity, and other unique space conditions for applications in biology, semiconductors, and optics.

3. Autonomous ISRU Manufacturing for Planetary Infrastructure

Autonomous In-Situ Resource Utilization (ISRU) systems that integrate extraterrestrial resource acquisition, processing, and manufacturing, creating a closed loop in which locally produced infrastructure enables access to additional resources and expands the resource base available to manufacturing beyond Earth. The initial process platform is glass additive manufacturing, motivated by the silicate-rich composition of lunar regolith and other rocky planetary materials.

Long-term direction

These three thrusts expand manufacturing along three dimensions: how directly human ideas can be realized, where manufacturing can occur, and what resources manufacturing can transform. The agenda progresses from focused, laboratory-scale demonstrations in each thrust toward integrated physical validation (hardware-in-the-loop ISAM systems, ground-based space analogs, and eventually flight-relevant prototypes), and, in parallel, toward larger collaborative programs spanning university, industry, and national laboratory partners.
